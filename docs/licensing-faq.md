A prepaid term still reassesses the band each year: moving up costs the discounted difference for the remaining years; moving down is not refunded.

## What counts in R and H?

**Funding counts.** R is all consolidated gross revenue plus investment/grant funding raised in the most recent 12 completed calendar months, including equity, capital contributions, convertible notes and commercial grants (grants for business activity, not core-purpose grants). Ordinary bank loans are excluded; loans from owners or investors count unless on ordinary arm's-length terms, and a loan converted into equity or forgiven counts then. Funding counts in the month it is received. Do not count valuation, profit or cash balance, and do not count a note again when converted or count an intra-group transfer twice. SAFEs and other instruments convertible into equity count like convertible notes. A raise counts in every month-end assessment whose 12-month window includes it, so a single round drops out about 12 months later. Funding does not cancel purpose-exempt education/public-benefit use.

Use consistent recognized local accounting principles or IFRS/GAAP for revenue. Deduct sales taxes and actual refunds, not operating expenses, model bills, commissions or payment fees. Customer advances enter when recognized as revenue, not twice. Principal-versus-agent accounting determines marketplace revenue; gross merchandise value is not automatically R. New businesses use actual figures since formation, without annualizing. Convert non-USD amounts at monthly average rates from a central bank or another consistent published source and keep the calculation.

**Count the whole current workforce, not software users:** full-/part-time employees, working founders, working officers/directors and regular unpaid business workers. Employees count immediately, including while on leave. Currently engaged assigned contractors/agency staff count after more than 30 worked calendar days in the preceding 12 months; casual unpaid helpers use that same test. Any work makes a worked day. Count from day 31 without retroactivity, remove genuinely ended engagements, and retain prior days if re-engaged within the rolling period. Count each natural person once, not fractional FTEs. Buying a service does not count the supplier's entire staff; identifiable assigned workers can count. Customers, enrolled students, passive investors and software agents do not count merely as such.

**Which group?** Include the beneficiary business and every entity controlling it, controlled by it or under common control, worldwide. Control means more than 50% voting power or actual power over management/policies, including by contract. Holdings of people acting together by agreement or arrangement, including nominees, are combined. Passive minority investment alone is not control. Founder-controlled siblings count together. Consolidate 100% of controlled entities, eliminate internal transactions, deduplicate people, and include acquired trailing history. New subsidiaries and installations do not reset thresholds. Shared sovereignty alone does not combine an entire country; special institutional rules separate exempt core work from commercial operations.

## Freelancers, employees and consultants

**Is freelance paid work free?** Only if your own group qualifies, or another applicable permission covers it. Paid own-account work is business use even without a registered company. A first employee does not by itself force payment: assess both R and H. A high-revenue solo business pays.

**Whose license covers client work?** The provider's, if Charness itself runs only on provider-controlled systems the client does not own, rent, fund/reimburse or control, and nobody on the client's side administers it or directs its agents. The provider's people producing the deliverable are not treated as client-side operators for this test. Merely accessing client repositories through separately licensed tools is not installing Charness there. A human request to a consultant is not agent direction if the consultant decides whether to pass it on; an issue that automatically triggers agent work is direction.

When installed for the client or directed by its staff, use the client's own free permission or group license, optionally procured by the provider. Short-term installers may administer it without automatically counting in H. An internal client instance may run on contractor infrastructure; ordinary administration of a client-controlled instance is not a marketed provider-controlled hosted-access service. Recurring hosted Charness access needs the separate hosting agreement.

**Does Charness take a cut of consulting?** No royalty on installation, configuration, workflows, maintenance, consulting or training. Those fees still enter the provider's R for its own use. The ordinary license does not promise those services from the licensor. Work as an employee remains the employer's use even on a personal laptop; an employer is not a freelance client.

## Assessments, trial and transitions

**When do we check?** Free groups assess R and current H each month end; first adoption uses the latest completed assessment (actual figures since formation if none exists). A first crossing by a genuinely free group starts a 90-calendar-day transition. Buy the then-applicable annual license, regain free eligibility, or stop covered use by the deadline. There is no back-payment for genuinely free use or authorized grace. Re-crossing within 12 months does not restart the original deadline.

**What if we are already above the line?** One group-wide evaluation of less than 32 consecutive calendar days is available, starting at first evaluation use. Productive work is allowed only as part of evaluation. People, departments, versions and pauses do not reset it. It is not followed by an extra 90-day growth trial and grants no onward-product/hosting rights.

**We already used MIT Charness.** Releases supplied under MIT stay MIT, without a retrospective bill. A group using MIT Charness before the first newly licensed release gets one 90-day internal-use transition starting when it first uses a release under the new terms. Then qualify free, buy, or stop the newly licensed version; continued use of MIT releases remains possible. Evaluation and this transition run concurrently, not sequentially. Charness never installs a new major version by itself: you decide when to move to the newly licensed releases.

**We grow after paying.** Ordinary organic growth, including a funding round, is covered until renewal (or the next anniversary of a prepaid term) without a surcharge. At renewal assess again; decreases permit a lower band or free eligibility. Mergers/acquisitions require combined-group reassessment within 90 days of completion (a free group joining a larger group has the same 90 days to bring it into compliance), with a positive fee difference prorated from completion for the remaining term and credit for overlapping prepaid coverage. A wrong original declaration must be corrected; it is not organic growth.

**What happens if we do not renew?** Paid organizational operation stops at term end unless independent free/MIT permission applies. You may retain source/backups for records and migration planning, not keep running a paid version forever. Your outputs are not removed. No keys or remote shutdown. Rights already granted in an executed agreement are not rewritten.

## Education and public benefit

**What is free?** Teaching, student learning and genuine academic/noncommercial research at recognized institutions or by individual researchers; also public/nonprofit educational administration and mission delivery, genuine charity and public-service work. Core budget, headcount, donations, genuine grants and other funding do not remove this permission. Ordinary tuition, taxes, appropriations, statutory fees and cost-recovery public services are not commercial triggers. Saving $2 million in government costs is not revenue. Classroom and genuine research participants may access the software without an OEM agreement.

**What is not automatically free?** Commercial R&D for an employer, proprietary paid-development contracts, product subscriptions, IP licensing and commercial data/services. A recognized private for-profit school has free teaching/learning, but ordinary group rules for commercial administration and other business.

**An exempt institution also trades.** Its commercial operation using Charness follows a special trigger: external commercial revenue C above $500,000 in the measurement period. While C is at most $500,000, assigned headcount alone does not trigger payment. Funding alone does not trigger C. Above C, use the operation's R (including its investment/commercial-grant funding, not exempt core grants) and assigned H to choose the ordinary higher band. Connected commercial operations are combined and shared material workers count once. Core teaching remains free. A university unit with $800,000 commercial revenue and ten workers pays $500 if no extra funding raises its band.

A separate for-profit spin-off or government-owned trading company follows ordinary R/H rules from the start. Aggregate related controlled commercial entities, not the institution's exempt core workforce/budget. Thus a 30-person spin-off at R $100,000 pays $500; a government trading company at R $12 million pays at least $6,000.

## Products, OEM and hosting

**We sell a product built using Charness.** If customers receive output, not Charness code/runtime/functionality, only your ordinary group eligibility applies. No output royalty or customer Charness license is required. Independent connectors and mere input/output automation do not automatically become OEM.

**We embed it in our product.** Before shipping or hosting that product, obtain a separate written OEM addendum listing products, architectures and product-specific tasks, even at a zero fee. Commercial terms section 17 provides the schedule and limited sublicensing terms. P is total trailing-12-month external embedded-product-family revenue, not investment, not a speculative Charness share. Include mandatory bundled charges, subtract actual refunds/taxes, not reseller commissions; exclude genuinely optional separately priced services. Related products cannot be split into new free thresholds.

| P | Annual OEM schedule amount |
|---|---:|
| P <= $500,000 | $0 |
| $500,000 < P <= $2,000,000 | $2,000 |
| $2,000,000 < P <= $10,000,000 | $6,000 |
| $10,000,000 < P <= $50,000,000 | $15,000 |
| $50,000,000 < P <= $250,000,000 | $30,000 |
| P > $250,000,000 | $60,000 |

Each paid OEM amount is one band above the ordinary table: the ordinary price of the next higher revenue band. P up to $500,000 stays $0.

**Supplier total = greater of ordinary group fee and OEM amount, not their sum.** Credit overlapping ordinary payments. R $1 million, H 12, P $800,000 means $2,000 total, not $2,500. A supplier at exactly R $1 billion with P $100,000 pays at least the $30,000 ordinary floor. Renewal/growth/acquisition rules also apply to OEM; the signed addendum must remain current for supplier operation, even if no-fee.

Customers need no separate Charness fee just to use the embedded component within the named product, whatever their size. They cannot extract it for unrelated automation or redistribute a platform. Valid installed on-premises runtime permission survives supplier expiry/refund/later breach; this narrow customer protection is not a perpetual supplier license. Expiry stops new distributions/updates and hosted operations. Customer size is not used for genuine OEM pricing. Supplier handles customer support and preserves legal notices.

**Can we sell hosted or rebranded Charness itself?** Only by a separate hosting/reseller agreement with per-client eligibility or normal group licenses. An OEM fee does not cover unlimited corporations buying substantially Charness itself. Pure administration for named clients uses their permissions; the host's separate internal use uses its own group band. There is no published blanket wholesale grant. Write to charnesshub@gmail.com.

## Sharing, payment and verification

**Can we modify or share it?** Modify for permitted or commercially licensed purposes. Share no-charge mirrors/forks with the required terms/notices and unchanged recipient eligibility. Authorized client installation can be charged as a service. Selling copies or embedding them needs separate rights; it is not unrestricted paid redistribution. Commercial internal modifications may be shared only on the same limited pass-through basis specified in the commercial terms.

**Is it open source?** New releases under these terms are source-available, not OSI open source. Every release before 4.0 was published under MIT and remains MIT. GitHub may classify the custom license as "Other".

**How do we buy?** There is no online checkout: write to charnesshub@gmail.com (information: https://olosegres.github.io/charness/commercial/). We reply with a written order and invoice stating group scope, assessment/bands, fixed terms, term dates, list price, any discount and its condition, net fee and taxes. No user quantity is ordered. For procurement or custom terms, contact the same address.

**Refunds and referrals?** Full refund within 30 days after any payment, including renewal or a multi-year prepayment (no pro-rata refund of a prepayment after those 30 days; one such no-reason refund per group in any 12 months); corresponding paid rights end, subject to independent rights and valid installed OEM-customer protection. Approved partners under a separate agreement earn 20% of collected license fees (after any discount) for referred new customers' first 12 months, excluding taxes/refunds/chargebacks. No automatic renewal commission, self-referrals or procurement kickbacks; this is separate from consulting income.

**No keys means no verification?** No. This is honour-based: no compulsory registration for ordinary free use, keys, activation, compliance telemetry or remote shutdown. Keep eligibility records for three years. A credible discrepancy first leads to a written officer/accountant certification request with 30 days to respond. Only unresolved reasonably grounded discrepancies may lead to a confidential independent audit, at most annually with at least 30 days' notice, relevant records only and redacted confirmations accepted where sufficient. No access to code repositories, prompts, credentials, customer data or production systems. The licensor pays unless underpayment exceeds BOTH 10% and $1,000; recoverable audit costs are capped at recovered underpayment. The public license binds free organizational claimants too; hobbyists' private affairs are excluded. First-notice cure remains 32 days; honest corrections and agreed payment plans are possible, not penalty multipliers.

**Does this include model subscriptions or support?** No. Obtain and pay for provider access appropriate to your intended use and follow provider terms; a shared Charness instance does not authorize sharing consumer accounts. Team, Enterprise or API offerings may be relevant, but check their actual permissions. Hosting, setup, consulting, support and SLAs are not included.
